How StarSet is built

From a college project to a sky you can hold.

StarSet started as a BSc project: an astronomy site for kids. This page is the product write-up behind the rebuild — who it’s for, what was required, how it’s put together, how we know the numbers are right, and what we chose not to do.

1 · The problem

Why another astronomy site?

Kids learn the sky from flat pictures

Textbook diagrams squash distances and freeze time, so “why do we have seasons?” or “why isn’t Pluto a planet?” never quite click.

Indian sky culture is missing

Families plan Purnima, Ekadashi and Sankranti by the Moon and Sun, but no sky app shows where a tithi or nakshatra actually is overhead.

Great tools are fragmented

Stellarium does the sky, Google Earth does the planet, NASA does missions. Nobody connects them for a 12-year-old, in their language, on a phone.

4 · Architecture

Static pages and shared data files. All the maths runs in the browser.

5 · Astro, the AI guide

From my MSc chatbot to a guide that can show you the answer.

My MSc Data Science project was the StarSet Chatbot, an NLP chatbot that answered astronomy questions. Astro is its successor: the same idea, now built into every StarSet page and powered by a large language model (Claude). The goal stayed the same: let a curious kid ask “why?” in their own words and get a clear answer. What changed is that Astro can also show the answer.

Ask

Type or speak, in English, हिंदी, Hinglish, मराठी or मारवाड़ी.

Add context

The page tells Astro what’s on screen: the focused planet, the simulation date, your city, and which planets are above your horizon right now.

Answer

A short, accurate answer streams in, in the language you used. Tap Listen to hear it.

Show

If you asked to see something, Astro flies the 3D view, points the sky or opens the right page.

Design choices

Maths from the engine, words from the AI

Positions, rise times and eclipses still come from Astronomy Engine. Astro reads them from the page instead of guessing, and is told never to invent eclipse dates.

Your language, your script

Ask in Hinglish and the reply comes back in Hinglish. Ask in Devanagari and it answers in Devanagari. The Listen button picks a Hindi or English voice to match.

Made for kids

Short answers with one everyday comparison, focused on space, suitable for children, with a visible “Astro is an AI and can make mistakes” note.

StarSet keeps nothing

Questions go to Claude only to get the answer. StarSet keeps no copy: the chat lives in your browser tab and is gone when you close it. No accounts, no StarSet server.

Works without AI too

Where Claude isn’t available (for example on GitHub Pages), Astro switches to Astro Lite: an offline guide that understands English, Hinglish and हिंदी with keyword rules, answers from StarSet’s own planet data, and can still fly the 3D view and point the sky — much like the original MSc chatbot.

Fits phones

On a phone the chat opens full-screen, and it gets out of the way after it shows you something, so you see Saturn and not the chat.

Tested in a headless browser with a stand-in AI: streamed answers, each tool on and across pages, the chat following you to the next page, and layouts at 1440 px, 390 px and phone landscape.

7 · Key decisions

No backend. Every page is one HTML file plus shared data. That means zero hosting cost, nothing to maintain, and nothing collected about children. The trade-off: no accounts, so messages and settings live only in your browser.
Compute, don’t download. Planet positions, eclipses and the panchang are calculated on the device with Astronomy Engine. That avoids a dependency on any API that could change, rate-limit or disappear.
Licences first. Only CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or public-domain images are used, all credited in the footer. A few beautiful moon maps were left out because they were non-commercial or unlabelled.
Degrade gracefully. Where a viewer blocks something (the phone compass, live street tiles, search), the page explains it and offers a manual fallback, such as compass buttons or an offline globe.
An AI guide on top, not instead. Astro explains and navigates, but every number on screen is still computed. The AI is a friendly front door to the engine, not a replacement for it.
Marwari as a first-class language. No sky app speaks Marwari. It shares a voice with Hindi for speech, and its text was written for review by a native speaker.
